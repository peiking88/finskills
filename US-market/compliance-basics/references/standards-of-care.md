# Standards of Care Comparison

## The Three-Tier System

US securities regulation operates on three tiers of client protection, depending on the relationship type.

## Tier 1: Investment Adviser Fiduciary Duty (IA Act Section 206)

**Who:** SEC-registered investment advisers (RIAs) and their representatives.

**Standard:** Supreme Court interpretation (*SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau*, 1963) of Sections 206(1) and 206(2) as imposing a federal fiduciary duty.

### Duty of Care
- Provide advice in the client's best interest based on understanding of client objectives
- Seek best execution when selecting broker-dealers
- Provide ongoing advice and monitoring throughout the relationship (not just at point of recommendation)

### Duty of Loyalty
- Must not place own interests ahead of client's
- Full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts — sufficiently specific for informed consent
- Even with disclosure and consent, cannot act inconsistently with client's best interest

### Key Characteristics
- **Ongoing:** Applies throughout the advisory relationship, not just at point of recommendation
- **Proactive:** Must monitor and update advice as circumstances change
- **Conflicts:** Must eliminate or disclose with specific, informed consent
- **Compensation:** Fee-based (AUM, hourly, fixed) — must disclose all compensation arrangements

## Tier 2: Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)

**Who:** SEC-registered broker-dealers and their associated persons, when making recommendations to retail customers.

**Standard:** SEC Regulation Best Interest (17 CFR 240.15l-1), effective June 30, 2020.

### Four Component Obligations

**1. Disclosure Obligation**
- Deliver Form CRS (Relationship Summary) at or before first recommendation
- Disclose material facts about the relationship, capacity, fees, and conflicts
- Must be "full and fair" — not boilerplate

**2. Care Obligation**
- Exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill
- Understand the product (risks, rewards, costs)
- Consider the customer's investment profile
- Evaluate reasonably available alternatives
- No excessive costs, excessive trading, or excessive risk

**3. Conflict of Interest Obligation**
- Identify all material conflicts
- Disclose and/or mitigate conflicts
- For conflicts creating incentives to place firm/personal interest ahead of customer: mitigation required (disclosure alone insufficient)
- Eliminate sales contests and product-specific bonuses

**4. Compliance Obligation**
- Written policies and procedures for all three substantive obligations
- Training programs
- Supervisory systems
- Documentation of recommendation basis
- Periodic testing and review

### Key Characteristics
- **Point-of-recommendation:** Applies when a recommendation is made, not continuously
- **Retail customers only:** Does not apply to institutional recommendations
- **Allows conflicts if mitigated:** Does not require elimination of all conflicts
- **Commission-based compensation permitted:** With appropriate disclosure and mitigation

## Tier 3: FINRA Suitability (Rule 2111)

**Who:** FINRA member broker-dealers (baseline standard; Reg BI raises the bar above this).

### Three Suitability Obligations

**1. Reasonable-Basis Suitability**
- Understand the product before recommending it to anyone
- Perform reasonable diligence on risks, rewards, and features

**2. Customer-Specific Suitability**
- Recommendation must fit the particular customer's investment profile
- Profile factors: age, financial situation, tax status, objectives, experience, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, other investments

**3. Quantitative Suitability**
- A series of recommended transactions must not be excessive
- Key metrics: turnover ratio (concern > 6), cost-to-equity ratio (concern > 20%)
- Applies to accounts under the representative's control

### Customer Investment Profile Elements

| Factor | What to Gather |
|--------|---------------|
| Age | Date of birth |
| Financial situation | Income, net worth, liquid net worth, expenses, liabilities |
| Tax status | Marginal bracket, account types |
| Investment objectives | Preservation, income, growth, speculation |
| Time horizon | Short (<3yr), intermediate (3–10yr), long (>10yr) |
| Liquidity needs | Anticipated cash needs, emergency reserves |
| Risk tolerance | Willingness and capacity for loss |
| Investment experience | Familiarity with product types and strategies |
| Other investments | Existing holdings, concentration, held-away assets |

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Dimension | Fiduciary (IA) | Reg BI (BD) | Suitability (FINRA) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|---------------------|
| When it applies | Ongoing relationship | Point of recommendation | Point of recommendation |
| Client interests | Must always come first | Must not place BD interest ahead | Must be suitable |
| Conflicts | Eliminate or specific informed consent | Disclose + mitigate | Disclose material conflicts |
| Cost consideration | Best execution required | Consider cost, no excessive costs | Cost reasonableness |
| Alternatives | Must seek best available | Must consider reasonably available | Not explicitly required |
| Monitoring | Ongoing duty | Not required (unless agreed) | Not required |
| Compensation | Fee-based, disclose all | Commission permitted with disclosure | Commission permitted |

## ERISA Fiduciary Standard

Applies to retirement plan fiduciaries (ERISA Section 404):

- **Prudent expert rule:** Act with care, skill, prudence of a knowledgeable professional (higher than "prudent person")
- **Exclusive benefit rule:** Act solely in interest of plan participants
- **Diversification:** Diversify unless clearly imprudent not to
- **Prohibited transactions (Section 406):** No self-dealing, no transactions with parties in interest
- **PTE 2020-02:** Provides exemption pathway for commission-based compensation on rollover recommendations, conditional on acting in customer's best interest

## Dual Registrant Considerations

Professionals registered as both BD and IA must:

1. Clearly identify which capacity they are acting in for each interaction
2. Deliver appropriate disclosures (Form CRS covers both, but additional IA disclosures required)
3. Apply the correct standard of care based on the capacity
4. Document the capacity in which each recommendation was made
5. Implement supervisory procedures for each capacity separately

## What Constitutes a "Recommendation"

Under both Reg BI and suitability rules, a recommendation is not limited to "buy this stock." It includes:

- Explicit recommendations ("You should buy X")
- Account type recommendations (brokerage vs advisory, margin, options)
- Rollover recommendations (401k → IRA specifically flagged by SEC)
- Hold recommendations (explicit "hold" triggers obligations)
- Investment strategy recommendations (asset allocation, leverage, concentration)

General education, broad market commentary, and responses to unsolicited orders generally do NOT trigger these standards.
