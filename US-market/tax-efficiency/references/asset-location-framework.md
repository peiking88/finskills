# Asset Location Framework

## Core Principle

Place assets in the account type that minimizes lifetime tax burden. Asset allocation (the what) comes first; asset location (the where) optimizes around it.

## Optimal Placement Guide

| Asset Type | Best Account | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Bonds, REITs, TIPS | Tax-deferred (IRA/401k) | High ordinary income taxed at top rate |
| High-growth equities | Roth IRA | Tax-free growth on biggest winners |
| Index equity, municipal bonds | Taxable | LTCG rates, qualified dividends, muni exemption |
| Commodities, MLPs | Tax-deferred | Avoid Schedule K-1 and collectibles rates |
| High-turnover active funds | Tax-deferred | STCG taxed as ordinary income |
| International equity | Taxable | Foreign tax credit available |

## Tax Treatment by Account Type

### Taxable Brokerage
- Interest: Ordinary income rates
- Qualified dividends: 0%/15%/20% + 3.8% NIIT
- STCG (held ≤ 1 year): Ordinary income rates
- LTCG (held > 1 year): 0%/15%/20% + 3.8% NIIT
- Tax-loss harvesting available
- Step-up in basis at death

### Traditional IRA / 401(k) (Tax-Deferred)
- Contributions: Pre-tax (deductible)
- Growth: Tax-deferred
- Withdrawals: Taxed as ordinary income
- RMDs required starting at age 73
- No tax-loss harvesting benefit

### Roth IRA / Roth 401(k) (Tax-Exempt)
- Contributions: After-tax
- Growth: Tax-free
- Withdrawals: Tax-free (if account open 5+ years and age 59½+)
- No RMDs during owner's lifetime
- Best for highest-growth assets — all appreciation escapes tax permanently

### HSA (Triple Tax Advantage)
- Contributions: Pre-tax or deductible
- Growth: Tax-free
- Qualified medical withdrawals: Tax-free
- After age 65: Withdrawals for any purpose taxed as ordinary income (like Traditional IRA)
- Best use: Invest aggressively, pay medical expenses out-of-pocket, let it compound

## Asset Location Benefit Calculation

```
Annual Tax Savings = Sum across accounts of:
    (Tax_drag_if_placed_wrong − Tax_drag_if_placed_right)
```

The benefit increases with:
- Larger spread between ordinary income and LTCG rates
- Larger tax-advantaged account balances relative to total portfolio
- Higher-yielding bonds (more tax-inefficient in taxable)
- Longer investment horizon

## Worked Example

**Given:** $500K taxable + $500K Traditional IRA. Target: 50% bonds (5% yield) + 50% equities (10% return, 2% qualified dividends). Marginal rates: 32% ordinary, 15% LTCG.

**Naive (50/50 in each account):**
- Taxable bonds: $250K × 5% × 32% = $4,000
- Taxable equity dividends: $250K × 2% × 15% = $750
- Total tax drag: **$4,750/year**

**Optimal (bonds in IRA, equity in taxable):**
- Taxable equity dividends: $500K × 2% × 15% = $1,500
- Total tax drag: **$1,500/year**

**Annual savings: $3,250** (0.325% of total portfolio). Over 20 years compounded, this adds significantly to after-tax wealth.

## Common Mistakes

- **Placing bonds in taxable while equities sit in IRA.** This is the most common and most costly error.
- **Putting municipal bonds in tax-advantaged accounts.** Muni interest is already tax-exempt — no benefit from tax deferral.
- **Ignoring state taxes.** A California investor at 37% federal + 13.3% state faces a 50.3% STCG rate vs 0% in Texas/Florida.
- **Over-optimizing location at the expense of simplicity.** The benefit is real but modest (0.3–0.75%/year). Don't create a portfolio so complex that rebalancing becomes impractical.
